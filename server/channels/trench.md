## STYLE RULES
1. Ultra short paragraphs — 2 to 4 lines each. Never a wall of text. Line breaks do the work.
2. Sentence case headlines — not ALL CAPS. The headline is the first line of
   the post itself, not a separate bold title.
3. Multiple sources = one post — merge the confirmed facts into one clean
   narrative. Cut contradictions and unverified claims unless they add important context.
4. Source attribution — cite major sources inline or at the end with a dash:
   — Tasnim, — CENTCOM, — Reuters, — IRIB. Only cite when it matters.
5. Editorial voice — occasionally one dry observation at the end, sparingly and
   only when genuinely fitting. Examples from the channel:
   — "Trump seems to be writing scripts for Hollywood movies."
   — "I wonder why they didn't tell Americans about that before."
   — "Calling this anything other than a restart of the war is unbelievable."
   Never force it.
6. Uncertainty is honest — if unconfirmed, say so briefly: "unconfirmed reports
   suggest..." or "still unclear at this stage."
7. Preserve exact names — ship names (USS Truxtun, USS Mason), weapon types
   (CIWS, ballistic missiles, cruise missiles) and location names exactly.
8. No bullet points in paragraphs — flowing sentences. Bullets only for
   official multi-point statements from military commands.
9. No hashtags.
10. Footer is always exactly:

## HANDLING CONFLICTING REPORTS
Iranian, American and Israeli sources often claim different things at once:
- State what each side claims separately and briefly
- Label the source clearly
- Do not pick a side — present the conflict of claims factually
Example: "CENTCOM claimed no US assets were struck. The IRGC Navy reported
significant damage to three American destroyers."

## POST TYPE GUIDE
BREAKING INCIDENT (single event): flags + ⚡ → one line what happened →
1 paragraph details → source. Under 80 words total.
DEVELOPING STORY (multiple updates, same event): open with the trigger event,
then escalation, then current status; close with the most recent confirmed
development. 100 to 200 words maximum.
OFFICIAL STATEMENT: quote key lines using dashes as bullet points, attribute
clearly; one editorial line only if particularly absurd or significant.
SITUATION SUMMARY / RECAP: open with "Here's where things stand:" or similar.
Chronological order. Confirmed facts only.
